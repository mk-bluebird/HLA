# Optional Identity and Discovery Preference Policy

## Purpose

This policy allows adults to self-describe optional identity facets and to set
optional discovery preferences without being required to disclose a legal,
medical, social, sexual, or protected identity.

## Core rules

1. Identity self-description is optional.
2. Discovery preferences are optional.
3. Neither identity nor discovery preference is consent.
4. Omission must not reduce platform access, visibility, dignity, or safety.
5. A member may revise, withdraw, or delete a self-description or preference at
   any time.
6. The platform must not infer missing facets.
7. The platform must not require a member to justify, prove, certify, or defend
   a self-description.
8. Identity data must not become a trust, authenticity, safety, risk,
   desirability, popularity, compatibility, or response-likelihood score.
9. Protected or sensitive identity claims must remain private by default.
10. A recipient must not see whether a facet is absent, hidden, withdrawn, or
    never created.

## Optional self-description areas

A member may optionally describe:

- Display name.
- Pronouns.
- Language.
- General identity terms selected by the member.
- Gender self-description.
- Sexuality or orientation self-description.
- Relationship orientation or relationship-style self-description.
- Cultural, faith, or community self-description.
- Optional species/otherkin or other self-expression facet.
- General profile narrative.

No listed area is required for ordinary participation.

## Optional discovery preferences

A member may optionally specify:

- Which self-described people they are open to viewing in a discovery context.
- Which current connection intents they want to explore.
- Which activity contexts they prefer.
- Which communication, pace, and public-first preferences matter to them.

Discovery preferences must not:

- Require disclosure of the member's own identity.
- Infer the member's identity from a preference.
- Exclude or rank people by protected identity.
- Treat a preference as permission to contact, sexualize, stereotype, or
  pressure another person.
- Override another member's visibility, boundary, consent, or block settings.

## Prohibited use

HLA must not use identity or discovery-preference data for:

- Advertising.
- Data brokerage.
- Person-level ranking.
- Compatibility scores.
- Safety, trust, risk, or reputation scores.
- Response prediction.
- Content moderation profiling.
- Law-enforcement or third-party disclosure without applicable process and
  documented policy.
- Default model training.
