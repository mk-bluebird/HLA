# HLA Free-Functionality Floor Policy

## Purpose

This policy ensures that every eligible Hearthline member has access to a fully
functional, low-token-cost HLA baseline for consent, privacy, accessibility,
ordinary discovery, conversation assistance, planning, safety controls, and
appeal.

A platform operator may offer optional paid features only when those features do
not create a second tier of social access, safety, visibility, autonomy,
accessibility, or entitlement.

## Free-functionality floor

The following must remain available without subscription, payment, purchase,
advertising exposure, identity disclosure, location sharing, camera access,
microphone access, voice use, or high-end device requirements:

- Account access and account recovery path.
- Privacy settings and privacy-scope review.
- Consent explanation, grant, decline, pause, withdrawal, and expiry review.
- Boundary Ledger and pace controls.
- Block, mute, restrict, report, correction, appeal, quiet exit, deletion, and
  export request.
- Text-first, keyboard, screen-reader, reduced-motion, low-bandwidth,
  no-camera, no-microphone, no-voice, and asynchronous paths.
- Current Connection Intent Card.
- Relationship Goals Canvas.
- Self-directed and chronological discovery of eligible options.
- Pause Discovery, hide, show fewer, and category controls.
- Explanation of why an option appeared.
- Broad future activity windows.
- Public-first and remote-first planning templates.
- Source-labeled public venue/transit information.
- Member-invoked low-token conversation drafting.
- Member-invoked topic suggestions.
- Member-invoked boundary, decline, pause, exit, and access-request drafting.
- Consent checkpoint explanation.
- Optional aftercare/decompression reflection.
- Plain-language policy and decision explanation.
- Member-owned decision history and appeal path.

## Free HLA request categories

Free HLA access must include reasonable request capacity for:

- Opening drafts.
- Reply drafts.
- Clarification drafts.
- Boundary drafts.
- Respectful decline drafts.
- Pause and quiet-exit drafts.
- Public-first and remote-first invitation drafts.
- Access-request drafts.
- Reviewed topic suggestions.
- Consent explanation.
- Policy explanation.
- Selected-text review within documented size limits.
- Privacy-scope explanation.
- Broad activity-window explanation.
- Public-resource and source-information explanation.
- Private reflection and planning prompts.

## Allowed operational limits

Operators may apply transparent, non-punitive operational controls:

- Per-request maximum input size.
- Per-member low-token request budget over a documented rolling period.
- Per-action automation-abuse rate limits.
- Queueing for non-urgent higher-cost tasks.
- Low-bandwidth/text-only fallback.
- Local-first drafting where feasible.
- Temporary service-degradation notice.
- Maximum file size and expiry for member-controlled media.

Every limit must:

- Be documented in plain language.
- Provide a non-punitive fallback where feasible.
- Avoid social visibility, safety, trust, or ranking effects.
- Avoid access discrimination based on payment, device, identity, or access path.
- Be reviewable through a member-accessible support or appeal path.

## Prohibited paywalls

Operators must not require payment for:

- Blocking, muting, restricting, reporting, appealing, deleting, exporting,
  pausing, or withdrawing consent.
- Privacy controls or privacy explanations.
- Accessibility paths or accommodations.
- Text-first, no-camera, no-microphone, no-voice, low-bandwidth, keyboard, or
  screen-reader access.
- Ordinary consent checkpoint explanations.
- Basic conversation drafting and boundary support.
- Basic self-directed discovery.
- Pause Discovery or hide/show-fewer controls.
- Broad future activity windows.
- Public-first or remote-first planning templates.
- Source-labeled public venue/transit information.
- Correction, human review request, or appeal.
- Member-owned data access.

## Prohibited paid advantages

Paid status must not:

- Improve a member's discovery rank or allocation.
- Purchase greater visibility to other members.
- Increase entitlement to replies or invitations.
- Bypass another member's boundary, block, consent checkpoint, or disclosure
  scope.
- Unlock a trust, safety, verification, reputation, desirability, popularity,
  compatibility, or response-likelihood advantage.
- Reveal location, presence, attendance, identity, or private data.
- Remove mandatory consent checkpoints.
- Create a priority-message, priority-reply, priority-date, or priority-intimacy
  mechanism.
- Affect moderation, appeal outcomes, or access to safety controls.
- Be used as a matching, ranking, risk, or social-value signal.

## Permitted optional paid features

Optional paid features may offer additional private compute or member-owned
storage only when a complete free path remains available.

Potential examples:

- Larger member-selected text input limits for private drafting.
- Longer local-only document transformation.
- Additional member-owned export formats.
- Additional device-local organization features.
- Higher quota for non-urgent optional private-compute tasks.
- Institutional hosting, tenancy administration, or support tooling that does
  not weaken member rights.

## Required operator adoption contract

An operator adopting HLA must:

- Preserve the free-functionality floor.
- Preserve all consent, privacy, accessibility, safety, exit, correction, and
  appeal controls.
- Publish any operator-specific paid feature policy.
- Prove paid status is excluded from discovery, ranking, matching, safety,
  moderation, and social-value systems.
- Provide a no-payment core path.
- Provide an accessible low-bandwidth/text-first path.
- Submit operator policy changes to rights/access review.
- Not use HLA-generated private content for advertising or default model
  training.
