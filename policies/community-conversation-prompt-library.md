# Community Conversation Prompt Library

## Purpose

The Community Conversation Prompt Library offers optional, low-pressure prompts
that members may use in community spaces, direct conversation, or private
reflection.

Prompts are invitations, not tests. A person may skip any prompt, answer in
their own words, choose a different prompt, or turn prompts off.

## Required prompt properties

Every prompt must include:

- Category.
- Plain-language prompt text.
- Skip-friendly wording or a visible skip control.
- Accessible response formats.
- Review status.
- Review-by date.
- Policy version.

## Prompt selection rules

- Members choose a category or opt into a random prompt from categories they
  selected.
- The system may not infer a prompt category from behavior, messages, identity,
  protected characteristics, emotional state, health, recovery, location, or
  social graph.
- The system may not optimize prompts for reply rate, disclosure depth, time
  spent, message volume, emotional intensity, or engagement.
- The system may not repeatedly show a declined or hidden prompt.
- Prompts may be entirely disabled.

## Local relevance boundary

A prompt may refer to a broad, member-selected context such as:

- “What kind of public activity do you enjoy?”
- “What helps a public setting feel comfortable for you?”
- “What is a low-cost afternoon you would enjoy?”

It must not refer to:

- A nearby place.
- A venue a member attended.
- A home/work neighborhood.
- A route.
- A current location.
- A local identity group.
- A local incident or emergency unless it is clearly sourced public information
  and separated from ordinary social prompts.

## Prohibited content

Do not create prompts about:

- Identity verification.
- Sexual history, sexual role, or explicit sexual acts.
- Health, diagnosis, disability, recovery, or substance history.
- Finances, housing, employment, debt, income, or basic needs.
- Trauma, abuse, family conflict, grief, crisis, or mental-health state.
- Immigration, criminal history, legal status, religion, or politics.
- Exact location, schedule, availability, workplace, home, or contact details.
- Intimate images, body details, or medical testing.
- Safety, risk, personality, morality, or compatibility assessment.
- Why a member declined, blocked, paused, or did not respond.

## Review process

- Every prompt requires policy and accessibility review before publication.
- Members may hide a prompt category, flag a prompt for review, or turn prompts
  off without consequence.
- A prompt flag triggers content review, not an automated judgment about the
  person who flagged it.
- Prompts must be reviewed after material policy changes and before their
  review-by date.
