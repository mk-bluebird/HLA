# Opt-In Research Access Program Policy

## Purpose

The Opt-In Research Access Program permits narrowly defined, separately approved
research studies using voluntary member participation and data-minimizing
methods.

It is not a default data commons, blanket anonymization program, product
analytics warehouse, or source of data for matching, advertising, moderation,
or model training.

## Study requirements

Every study must define:

- Study identifier and sponsor.
- Research question.
- Data categories requested.
- Why each category is necessary.
- Member-facing consent language.
- Eligibility and exclusion criteria that do not rely on inferred protected
  identity or vulnerability.
- Data minimization plan.
- Privacy methods.
- Re-identification risk assessment.
- Retention and destruction schedule.
- Withdrawal process and limits.
- Data recipients.
- Data-use agreement.
- Publication and aggregate-release plan.
- Small-cell suppression threshold.
- Independent ethics/privacy/accessibility review.
- Review-by date.

## Default exclusions

Studies must not use:

- Private messages.
- Intimate media.
- Calls or recordings.
- Exact or approximate location.
- Routes.
- Venue attendance.
- Contact books.
- Social graph.
- Profile views.
- Device identifiers.
- Biometric data.
- Identity claims.
- Protected identity.
- Health, disability, recovery, substance history, trauma, or diagnosis.
- Finances, housing, employment, legal, or immigration status.
- Reports, blocks, restrictions, appeals, or moderation records.
- Casual-encounter history.
- Relationship history.
- Person-level engagement metrics.

Any exception requires a separately documented, extraordinary justification,
specific member consent, independent review, strict segregation, and a
demonstrated reason why a less sensitive design cannot answer the question.

## Operational separation

Research data must not be used for:

- Discovery.
- Matching.
- Ranking.
- Moderation.
- Safety or risk scoring.
- Advertising.
- Pricing.
- Account eligibility.
- Feature access.
- Default model training.
- Person-level profiling.

## Aggregate release requirements

Any released aggregate must:

- Use a documented privacy method appropriate to the release.
- Apply small-cell suppression.
- Apply delayed publication where timing could reveal activity.
- Exclude direct and quasi-identifiers.
- Avoid location, timing, relationship, or narrative combinations that increase
  re-identification risk.
- Be reviewed before release.
- State limitations and uncertainty.

## Member-facing statement

> Research participation is optional. Declining does not affect Hearthline
> access. Each study explains what data it requests, why, who may receive it,
> how long it is kept, and how to withdraw future participation where possible.
