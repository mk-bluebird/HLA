# Communication and Comfort Controls Policy

## Purpose

Communication and Comfort Controls let a member choose practical interaction
conditions without requiring disclosure of neurodivergence, disability, health,
diagnosis, recovery, competence, vulnerability, or personal explanation.

## Core rules

1. Controls are optional and private by default.
2. A member may use all core Hearthline features without selecting any control.
3. A member may revise, pause, withdraw, or delete controls at any time.
4. A member may selectively disclose one control to one recipient for one
   purpose, subject to a separate disclosure scope.
5. A recipient must not see which controls a member omitted, withdrew, hid, or
   never created.
6. Controls are practical preferences, not a diagnosis, identity claim,
   capability judgment, relationship signal, or consent grant.
7. A control does not create an obligation for another person to continue,
   respond, disclose, meet, or accept an interaction.
8. A member's controls must not reduce their discovery visibility or access.

## Prohibited use

Hearthline must not use Communication and Comfort Controls for:

- Neurodivergence inference.
- Disability or health inference.
- Identity inference.
- Safety, risk, trust, popularity, desirability, or compatibility scoring.
- Discovery ranking or allocation.
- Advertising or data brokerage.
- Price differentiation.
- Moderation profiling.
- Behavioral research cohorts.
- Response prediction.
- Employment, housing, financial, or legal assessment.
- Default model training.

## Required access paths

Every control-management surface must work through:

- Keyboard-only navigation.
- Screen-reader-compatible semantics.
- Text-first interaction.
- Reduced-motion display.
- Low-bandwidth path.
- No-camera, no-microphone, and no-voice paths.
- Plain-language explanations.

## Member-facing text

> These are practical communication preferences. You do not need to explain why
> you use them. You can change or remove them at any time.
