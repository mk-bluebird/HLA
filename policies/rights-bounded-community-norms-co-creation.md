# Rights-Bounded Community Norms Co-Creation Policy

## Purpose

Rights-Bounded Community Norms Co-Creation lets members help shape
context-specific community practices while preserving non-negotiable rights,
privacy, accessibility, consent, anti-discrimination, non-commercial integrity,
and appeal protections.

Community participation is not a substitute for individual consent, platform
policy review, accessibility review, or legal/policy review where required.

## Governance hierarchy

```text
Rights floor
  > Platform policy
    > Context-specific community norm
      > Member-level preference
```

A lower layer must not weaken or override a higher layer.

## Rights floor

Every community context must preserve:

- Specific, informed, ongoing, revocable consent.
- Privacy by default and minimum necessary data use.
- Accessibility parity.
- Text-first access.
- Keyboard and screen-reader access.
- No-camera, no-microphone, no-voice paths.
- No location, route, attendance, or presence tracking.
- No protected-identity inference.
- No discrimination or harassment.
- Block, mute, restrict, report, correction, appeal, deletion, and quiet exit.
- No forced disclosure.
- No value-for-intimacy or dependency brokerage.
- No person-level scores or reputation.
- No autonomous social action.
- No default chat surveillance.

## Permitted community norms

A community may propose rules about:

- Discussion format.
- Topic order.
- Text-first or asynchronous defaults.
- Voluntary pause intervals between topics.
- Community prompt categories from an approved library.
- Courtesy and respectful-disagreement language.
- Public-source citation style.
- Community-event framing.
- Accessibility reminder language.
- Clear moderation-process explanation.

## Prohibited community norms

A community may not adopt rules that:

- Require identity, health, disability, recovery, financial, housing, employment,
  legal, immigration, religion, political, relationship, sexual, or intimate
  disclosure.
- Require a camera, microphone, voice, headset, location, travel, driving,
  biometrics, or device data.
- Require response, voting, attendance, participation, or explanation.
- Restrict block, report, quiet exit, deletion, correction, appeal, or consent
  withdrawal.
- Permit discrimination, harassment, stalking, doxxing, coercion, recording, or
  surveillance.
- Create a score, rating, leaderboard, reputation, trust mark, safety mark,
  popularity metric, or social-acceptance measure.
- Link social or sexual access to money, housing, employment, debt relief,
  transportation dependency, drugs, gifts, or basic needs.
- Treat majority approval as an individual's consent.

## Decision process

A proposed norm must:

1. Be published in plain language.
2. State scope, purpose, affected context, expiry/review date, and policy version.
3. Receive accessibility and rights-floor validation before member deliberation.
4. Offer a no-response and non-participation path.
5. Provide member comment or alternative wording path where feasible.
6. Be approved through the selected context process only after validation.
7. Be appealable.
8. Be reviewable after material changes.
9. Expire or require renewal on a documented schedule.

## Member-facing notice

> Community norms can shape how this space is organized, but they cannot remove
> your rights to privacy, accessibility, consent, block, report, appeal, quiet
> exit, or non-discriminatory treatment.
