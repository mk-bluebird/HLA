# HLA: Hearthline Assistant

**Version:** 0.1.0-design  
**Status:** Design specification; not production code  
**Audience:** Adults 30+, builders, researchers, accessibility reviewers, safety reviewers, policy reviewers, and moderators  
**Scope:** Consent-first connection, friendship, dating, romance, shared activities, community participation, and consensual adult intimacy  
**Legal notice:** This specification is not legal advice. Jurisdiction-specific policy must receive qualified legal review before deployment.

> HLA is a consent-first, privacy-preserving assistant for adults 30+ who want
> friendship, love, community, shared activities, dating, romance, and
> consensual adult intimacy at their own pace. HLA protects autonomy,
> accessibility, privacy, and equal dignity. It does not rank people by worth,
> desirability, safety, trust, compatibility, popularity, or likelihood of
> responding.

---

## 1. Purpose

HLA exists to reduce avoidable barriers to voluntary adult connection.

It supports adults who may want:

- Friendship and conversation.
- Shared public or remote activities.
- Dating and romance.
- Consensual casual connection or intimacy.
- Community participation.
- Low-pressure social planning.
- Privacy-preserving discussion of pace, boundaries, and access preferences.

HLA treats consensual adult intimacy and casual connection as legitimate forms of
adult connection. It does not treat intimate access, personal disclosure,
location, attention, or time as goods to be exchanged for money, housing,
employment, transportation dependency, debt relief, drugs, gifts conditioned
on intimacy, or basic needs.

HLA does not diagnose, assess, monitor, infer, or rank recovery, health,
disability, identity, sexuality, finances, housing, employment, emotion,
vulnerability, social value, or relationship worth.

---

## 2. Mission

HLA aims to:

- Support adults 30+ in finding voluntary, respectful connection.
- Make consent specific, informed, ongoing, and revocable.
- Reduce unnecessary disclosure of identity, routine, location, and sensitive
  personal context.
- Give members meaningful control over discovery, communication, pace, access,
  visibility, and interaction escalation.
- Support accessibility through text-first, keyboard, screen-reader,
  low-bandwidth, reduced-motion, asynchronous, no-camera, no-microphone, and
  no-voice paths.
- Provide clear block, mute, restrict, report, correction, appeal, deletion,
  and quiet-exit controls.
- Help builders create auditable, modular, policy-bounded social tools.
- Measure clarity, autonomy, accessibility, privacy preservation, and voluntary
  connection rather than engagement, disclosure, dates, purchases, or time spent.

## 4. Core principles

### 4.1 Consent is specific and renewable

Consent must be explicit, informed, freely given, ongoing, capability-specific,
recipient-specific, purpose-specific, and revocable.

Consent to one thing does not imply consent to another:

```text
A match is not consent to a message.
A message is not consent to a call.
A call is not consent to media.
Media is not consent to recording.
Conversation is not consent to sexual topics.
Sexual-topic discussion is not consent to touch or sex.
A meeting plan is not consent to travel, a private venue, recording, intimacy,
or continued contact.
```

Silence, delay, a profile field, a match, a reaction, an earlier consent grant,
a stated preference, a previous encounter, or past intimacy is not new consent.

### 4.2 Privacy is social safety

HLA collects the minimum data needed for a stated member-controlled purpose.

Sensitive information includes:

- Identity and identity presentation.
- Sexual and intimate preferences.
- Relationship context.
- Accessibility and communication preferences.
- Location, broad region, venue detail, route, and meeting-plan information.
- Messages, media, calls, recordings, and AR data.
- Device, camera, microphone, voice, motion, gaze, spatial map, and biometric data.
- Health, recovery, financial, employment, housing, legal, and vulnerability context.

Sensitive information must not be advertising inventory, default model-training
data, a transferable asset, or a hidden ranking input.

### 4.3 Accessibility is a core requirement

Every core feature must support:

- Text-first access.
- Keyboard-only operation.
- Screen-reader-compatible semantics.
- Reduced-motion operation.
- Low-bandwidth operation.
- No-camera path.
- No-microphone path.
- No-voice path.
- Asynchronous communication path.
- Plain-language explanations.
- Member-controlled notification cadence.

Access preferences are practical controls, not diagnoses, competence judgments,
or identity labels.

### 4.4 No person-level scoring

HLA must not create, display, store, or use:

- Safety scores.
- Risk scores.
- Trust scores.
- Reputation scores.
- Popularity scores.
- Desirability scores.
- Compatibility scores.
- Response-likelihood scores.
- Influence scores.
- Social acceptance scores.
- Relationship-readiness scores.
- Emotional-state scores.
- Vulnerability scores.
- Protected-identity confidence scores.

HLA may use transparent, member-selected constraints to determine whether a
specific voluntary interaction option may be presented.

### 4.5 Members control pace and disclosure

Members may revise, pause, narrow, broaden, withdraw, or delete their:

- Connection intent.
- Activity preferences.
- Relationship goals.
- Pace preferences.
- Discovery visibility.
- Access and comfort controls.
- Identity presentation.
- Boundary preferences.
- Meeting-plan details.
- Trusted-contact plan.
- Consent grants.

No revision, withdrawal, pause, non-response, decline, block, report, or
deletion may reduce a member's dignity, access, or future visibility.

## 7. Architecture overview

HLA is modular and event-driven. Each module must expose a narrow service
contract, state purpose, define retention and deletion behavior, support
revocation, and reject forbidden input fields.

### 7.1 Core modules

| Module | Responsibility | Must not do |
|---|---|---|
| Adult Policy Core | Stores current adult-platform eligibility outcome | Expose DOB, legal name, verification method, or identity documents |
| Consent Core | Capability-scoped consent, expiry, revocation propagation | Treat preference or prior interaction as consent |
| Boundary Ledger | Stores member-selected yes/no/ask-first/not-discussing rules | Convert boundaries into risk, compatibility, or worth scores |
| Intent and Goals | Current intent, relationship goals, pace, activity context | Predict relationships, intimacy, or response |
| Discovery Core | Constraint-based broad-area and remote discovery | Use location, social graph, popularity, or behavior |
| Privacy Envelope | Recipient-scoped, expiring disclosure | Reveal live location, route, attendance, or identity by default |
| Meeting Planning | Public-first plan, venue category, independent-arrival preference | Track travel or guarantee safety |
| Trusted Contact Plan | Manual member-controlled check-ins | Infer emergencies or escalate automatically |
| Access Controls | Communication and planning preferences | Infer diagnosis or identity |
| Conversation Assistant | Member-invoked drafting and selected-text explanation | Scan all chats, diagnose, or enforce automatically |
| Coordination Drafts | Private editable invitation drafts | Send, negotiate, schedule, or consent autonomously |
| Virtual Contexts | Rules and access for bounded interaction spaces | Certify trust or retain participant rosters |
| Audit Trail | Policy/version/data-path accountability | Store protected identity, raw messages, or risk scores |
| Governance | Policy release, appeals, review, deletion evidence | Convert usage into surveillance profiles |

### 7.2 Trust zones

```text
Device zone
  Member-controlled browser, client, secure local storage, platform
  authenticator, accessibility settings, local drafts.

Identity zone
  Adult-policy eligibility result and credential lifecycle.

Consent zone
  Capability-scoped grants, withdrawals, expiry, recipient scope.

Connection zone
  Intent, goals, activity cards, discovery, meeting plans, conversation tools.

Privacy zone
  Identity claims, disclosure envelopes, venue capsules, retention controls.

Integrity zone
  Block, restrict, report, action-scoped abuse controls, human-review routing.

Governance zone
  Policy versions, audit projections, deletion evidence, appeal records,
  release evidence.

Integration zone
  Source-labeled public venue/transit references and documented external
  provider boundaries.
```

No zone may access another zone's sensitive data without an explicit,
least-privilege, purpose-limited service contract.

### 7.3 Implementation languages

Possible implementation languages and tools include:

- TypeScript for client flows, API contracts, schemas, and consent UI.
- Rust for privacy boundaries, cryptographic token verification, expiry, deletion,
  and WASM modules.
- Go for narrowly scoped high-throughput services where needed.
- Python for reviewed policy tooling, source maintenance, research aggregation,
  and static analysis.
- PostgreSQL with row-level security for purpose-limited relational state.
- Redis only for short-lived, revocable operational state.
- NATS or equivalent ephemeral event transport with revocation-aware consumers.
- OPA/Rego for policy enforcement.
- JSON Schema for API and object validation.
- GraphQL only for narrowly scoped member-owned data access.
- JSON-LD only for public, non-sensitive context definitions.
- WebCrypto or libsodium/WASM for client-side encryption where feasible.
- IndexedDB for local-first drafts and private reflection.

The initial implementation must not require Kafka, Neo4j, Elasticsearch, vector
databases, PostGIS, DID, verifiable credentials, zero-knowledge proofs, or
calendar integration. These may be evaluated later only when a narrow,
rights-preserving use case cannot be served by simpler auditable components.

### 9.2 Pause Discovery

Pause Discovery replaces “ghost mode.”

When enabled:

- Active discovery cards, goals, and activity windows leave new candidate

- The platform does not tell others that the member paused.
- The platform does not display “offline,” “hidden,” “nearby,” “inactive,” or
  “last active” information.
- The member may resume later without penalty.
- Existing blocks, reports, withdrawals, and consent restrictions remain active.

### 9.3 Member-requested reminders

Reminder defaults:

```text
Notifications: off
Recurrence: none
Escalation: none
Streaks: prohibited
Inactivity triggers: prohibited
```

A member may choose one-time, weekly, monthly, digest-only, in-app-only, or no
reminders. Dismissal has no penalty and does not increase reminder frequency.

## 10. Discovery and presentation

### 10.1 Constraint-based eligibility

HLA uses member-selected constraints, not person scores.

\[
E(a,b) =
A(a)
\land A(b)
\land C(a)
\land C(b)
\land I(a,b)
\land X(a,b)
\land P(a,b)
\land T(a,b)
\land R(a,b)
\land \neg B(a,b)
\land N(a,b)
\]

Where:

- \(A(x)\): adult-policy eligibility and active discovery state.
- \(C(x)\): current discovery consent.
- \(I(a,b)\): current intent overlap.
- \(X(a,b)\): shared activity or conversation context.
- \(P(a,b)\): compatible selected pace and boundary constraints.
- \(T(a,b)\): broad future activity-window overlap.
- \(R(a,b)\): matching broad region or mutual remote context.
- \(B(a,b)\): block, restriction, withdrawal, or policy boundary.
- \(N(a,b)\): non-commercial integrity requirement satisfied.

No term may use exact location, distance, route, venue attendance, contacts,
social graph, private messages, profile views, response speed, popularity,
identity, health, finances, housing, employment, disability, recovery,
vulnerability, image quality, biometrics, or predicted outcome.

### 10.2 Presentation allocation

When eligible options exceed display capacity, HLA must:

- Respect member-selected filters.
- Offer self-directed browsing.
- Offer chronological browsing where applicable.
- Use transparent rotation.
- Prevent repeat presentation after a member hides an option.
- Support one-at-a-time and low-stimulation presentation modes.
- Explain each result in plain language.
- Allow members to disable recommendations.

HLA must not order people using proximity, popularity, response rate, profile
completion, image activity, payment, time spent, social graph, person-level
trust, safety, risk, compatibility, or desirability.

### 10.3 Required explanation

Every presented option must offer:

> This option is based only on current settings you and another member selected,
> such as shared activity context, current intent, selected broad area or remote
> setting, and broad future time blocks. It does not use contacts, mutual
> friends, exact location, location history, venue visits, live presence,
> private messages, profile views, popularity, or response likelihood.

## 14. AI assistance and automation boundaries

### 14.1 Member-invoked assistance

HLA may provide optional assistance when a member explicitly asks for it:

- Boundary drafting.
- Respectful decline drafting.
- Public-first invitation drafting.
- Pace-request drafting.
- Consent-checkpoint explanation.
- Selected-text pressure-pattern explanation.
- Selected-text report drafting.
- Private planning prompts.
- Plain-language policy explanation.

The member may edit, discard, or save a draft. No assistant output creates an

### 14.2 Prohibited AI behavior

HLA must not:

- Monitor all messages by default.
- Analyze full conversations without member selection.
- Infer health, recovery, emotion, trauma, identity, disability, finances,
  vulnerability, or sexual intent.
- Diagnose a person.
- Determine whether someone is safe, unsafe, predatory, truthful, manipulative,
  legally liable, compatible, or likely to respond.
- Automatically report, block, restrict, suspend, notify, or message.
- Generate a person-level score.
- Use private content for discovery, advertising, behavior profiling, or default
  model training.
- Pressure a member to disclose, meet, continue contact, forgive, or engage in
  intimacy.

### 14.3 No agent-to-agent social negotiation

HLA must not support agent-to-agent negotiation for:

- Consent.
- Boundaries.
- Availability.
- Identity disclosure.
- Sexual topics.
- Meetings.
- Location.
- Venue selection.
- Transportation.
- Money or material exchange.
- Relationship terms.

A member-owned assistant may prepare a private draft using only inputs the
member actively selected. The assistant cannot send, accept, decline, schedule,
or negotiate.

```text
externalActionAuthorized = false
```

must remain true for all assistant-generated drafts.

## 15. Research and accountability

### 15.1 Allowed research

Research participation must be:

- Opt-in.
- Purpose-specific.
- Revocable where technically and legally feasible.
- Data-minimized.
- Time-limited.
- Subject to documented retention and deletion.
- Reviewed for privacy, accessibility, and member rights.

Permitted aggregate questions include:

- Can members understand why an option appeared?
- Can members find pause, hide, block, report, and withdrawal controls?
- Does keyboard-only access work?
- Does screen-reader access work?
- Does text-first access work?
- Does revocation propagate successfully?
- Do members understand consent checkpoints?
- Does a feature increase clarity or pressure?

### 15.2 Prohibited research use

HLA must not:

- Create identity, health, recovery, finance, housing, disability, sexuality, or
  vulnerability cohorts through inference.
- Measure sexual disclosure, intimate media sharing, meeting completion, dates,
  reply rate, time spent, purchases, or engagement as product success.
- Sell, transfer, advertise with, or default-train on sensitive connection data.
- Build user-level psychological, social, relationship, safety, or risk profiles.

### 15.3 Community Climate Signals

HLA may produce aggregate, thresholded, delayed signals for product review:

- Explanation comprehension.
- Pause and withdrawal-control discoverability.
- Block/report/appeal-path discoverability.
- Accessibility-path completion.
- Consent-revocation propagation.
- Interface-confusion indicators.

These signals must not contain member identifiers, counterpart identifiers,
protected identity, location, private messages, report contents, attendance,
engagement counts, or person-level outcomes.

## 16. Jurisdiction and policy review

HLA may be deployed only with jurisdiction-specific policy review appropriate to
the deployment context.

For Arizona-oriented deployments, policy review must address:

- Adult-only product access.
- Consent and non-coercion boundaries.
- Non-commercial integrity.
- Reporting and human-review obligations.
- Privacy, retention, deletion, and appeal.
- Venue/transit/source-information claims.
- Accessibility obligations.
- Material policy and statutory changes.

HLA must not tell a member:

```text
This is legal.
This is illegal.
You can legally consent.
This person is legally safe.
You may proceed.
```

HLA may instead say:

```text
This Hearthline feature is unavailable under the current platform policy.
This request requires policy or human review.
This information is not legal advice.
```

## 17. Security and data governance

### 17.1 Required controls

- Passkey-first authentication where feasible.
- Least-privilege service authorization.
- Purpose-limited access control.
- Encryption in transit and at rest.
- Field-level disclosure controls.
- Recipient-scoped access tokens.
- Short-lived capability tokens.
- Immediate revocation propagation.
- Expiry enforcement.
- Content-minimized accountability events.
- Row-level security where applicable.
- Schema validation that rejects unknown/prohibited fields.
- Deletion verification across operational stores, caches, indexes, queues,
  analytics projections, and training pipelines.

### 17.2 Data minimization rules

Do not store in ordinary connection/discovery systems:

- Government identity documents.
- Date of birth.
- Exact location.
- Routes.
- Location histories.
- Venue attendance.
- Contact books.
- Social graph.
- Private message corpus.
- Voiceprints, face templates, biometric templates, or emotion signals.
- Health, recovery, financial, housing, employment, legal, or vulnerability data.
- Popularity, reputation, safety, trust, risk, compatibility, or desirability scores.

### 17.3 Retention classes

Every data class must define:

```text
purpose
consent scope
access control
retention duration
expiry behavior
deletion behavior
correction mechanism
appeal/accountability path
material-change review
```
